1. Problem Definition & Practical Motivation

A citizen asks a question in plain Khmer — can deep learning retrieve the exact statutory article out of 1,976?

Task Definition (§5.A)

Input: Unstructured legal question in natural Khmer ($q$).

Output: Top-$k$ ranked statutory articles $\{d_1, \dots, d_k\}$ from the full 1,976-article corpus without statute/chapter filter hints.

«តើកិច្ចសន្យាបង្កើតឡើងដោយរបៀបណា?»

("How is a contract formed under the Civil Code?")

→ Ground Truth: Civil Code Art. 336 (ការបង្កើតកិច្ចសន្យា...) & Art. 311 (និយមន័យនៃកិច្ចសន្យា)

Why Lexical Keyword Search (BM25) Fails

  • Severe Vocabulary Mismatch (57.5% of BM25 misses): Citizens ask in conversational phrasing; statutes use formal civil law terminology.
  • Misleading Term Distraction: BM25 matches common query words like "របៀប" (manner) and retrieves irrelevant procedural articles (e.g., Art. 623 "Manner of dividing proceeds") while completely missing Art. 336.
  • Dense Semantic Representation: Neural bi-encoders map queries and statutes into a shared latent geometry, bridging vocabulary discrepancies via contextual meaning.

2. Dataset & Preprocessing: The Limon Font Challenge

Official government PDFs were typed in legacy 1990s ASCII fonts — extraction had to be engineered from scratch

The Legacy Limon Problem

  • Corpus Source: Official PDFs from Open Development Cambodia (Civil Code 2007: 1,304 articles, Criminal Code 2009: 672 articles).
  • Obsolete ASCII Encoding: Text layer contains raw Latin keystrokes (e.g. maRta 336>- karbegáItkic©snñ...). Standard PDF tools produce unreadable gibberish.
  • Visual Grapheme Inversion: Khmer pre-base vowels (េ ែ ៃ) and subscript consonants are typed *before* the base consonant visually, violating Unicode logical character ordering.

Engineered Solution & Validation

  • Custom Pipeline: Engineered LimonPdfExtractor with byte-level font mapping and a finite-state machine for syllable reordering into Unicode.
  • 100% Consecutive Extraction: Cleanly extracted all 1,976 articles (Civil 1–1304, Criminal 1–672) with zero missing provisions, audited against physical gazettes.
  • Structural Metadata: Preserved full hierarchical breadcrumbs (Book → Title → Chapter → Section → Article Number).

3. Training, Validation & Test Splits (Zero Leakage)

Rigorous split quarantine and data sanitization protocol enforced across all models (Seed 42)

Weak Supervision Data Generation

  • Title-to-Body Pairs: Because labeled Khmer legal QA datasets do not exist, 1,472 unique article titles served as natural query-body training pairs.
  • Ambiguous Titles Excluded: 270 articles sharing identical titles (e.g., "Definitions", "Competence") were quarantined to prevent ambiguous label noise.
  • Passage Title Stripping: Titles were strictly removed from passages during training to prevent trivial lexical copy-matching.

Benchmark Splits & Leakage Guard (§5.A)

  • Fixed Splits (Seed 42): 1,176 Train (80%) · 148 Validation (10%) · 148 Held-Out Test Titles (T-T Secondary Benchmark).
  • Primary T-Q Benchmark: 200 authentic human legal questions (100 Civil + 100 Criminal).
  • Zero Test Leakage: All 200 questions and their 261 relevant statutory articles were strictly quarantined from training. Test sets evaluated strictly once.

5. Training Process & InfoNCE Contrastive Loss

Shared contrastive learning formulation with in-batch negatives and symmetric cross-entropy

Symmetric InfoNCE Objective

Both query-to-document and document-to-query contrastive losses are averaged across batch size $B$:

L = -1/(2B) * Σ [ log( e^(sim(q_i,d_i)/τ) / Σ_j e^(sim(q_i,d_j)/τ) ) + log( e^(sim(d_i,q_i)/τ) / Σ_j e^(sim(d_j,q_i)/τ) ) ]

  • Temperature $\tau = 0.05$: Sharply penalizes hard negative false positives.
  • In-Batch Negatives: $B-1$ negative articles per sample without mining overhead.

Optimization & Engineering Controls

  • Optimizer: AdamW with linear warmup (10% of total steps) and cosine learning rate decay.
  • Regularization: Weight decay (0.01) + embedding dropout + contrastive repulsion.
  • Checkpointing (§5.B): torch.save saves model weights, optimizer, scheduler, and RNG state each epoch (last.pt, best.pt) with --resume flag support.

10. Conclusions & Defense Summary

Three key takeaways, engineering rigor, and transition to Q&A (§6.2)

1

Pre-training is Indispensable: Models trained from scratch (BiLSTM) fail outright on small legal data (0.1724 MRR@10) due to vocabulary sparsity; multilingual pretraining provides necessary structural priors.

2

Full Fine-Tuning is Decisive: Adapting all transformer layers (A3) is strictly required to surpass keyword search on conversational legal queries (+44.1% relative MRR@10 over BM25, p < 0.001).

3

Reproducible Engineering: 100% consecutive Limon extraction (1,976 articles), zero test leakage, 136 passing automated unit tests, and GPL-3.0 open-source code.
