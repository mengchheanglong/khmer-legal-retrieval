1. The Legal Retrieval Problem

A citizen asks a question in plain Khmer — can deep learning retrieve the exact statutory article out of 1,976?

The Retrieval Challenge

Khmer question → the correct statutory article, out of the full 1,976-article corpus, with zero statute or chapter hints.

"តើកិច្ចសន្យាបង្កើតឡើងដោយរបៀបណា?"

("How is a contract formed under the Civil Code?")

→ Civil Code Art. 336 (ការបង្កើតកិច្ចសន្យាដោយសំណើ និង ស្វីការ) & Art. 311 (និយមន័យនៃកិច្ចសន្យា)

Why Keyword Search (BM25) Fails

  • Vocabulary mismatch (57.5% of BM25 failures): citizens ask in conversational phrasing; statutes use formal legal taxonomy. BM25 gets distracted by question words like "របៀប" and retrieves irrelevant procedural articles.
  • Corpus Engineering: Official PDFs were typeset in obsolete 1990s Limon ASCII fonts. Built custom Limon→Unicode converter with logical reordering, achieving 100% consecutive extraction (1,304 Civil + 672 Criminal).

2. Dataset & Four Architectures

Same corpus, same fixed split (seed 42), same InfoNCE contrastive loss — only encoder architecture varies

Rigorous Experimental Controls

  • 1,976 official Khmer articles (Civil Code 1,304 + Criminal Code 672).
  • Zero test leakage: 200 real human test questions (and all 261 cited articles) strictly protected from training; titles stripped from passages; evaluated once.

All models share the InfoNCE contrastive loss (τ = 0.05): pulls the ground-truth legal article closer to the question embedding and repels in-batch negatives.

5. Conclusions & Defense Summary

Three key takeaways, engineering rigor, and transition to Q&A

1

Pre-training is non-negotiable at small legal scale (~1.1k pairs) — from-scratch BiLSTM (A1) overfits and fails outright (0.17 MRR@10).

2

Full fine-tuning (A3) bridges the citizen-to-statute vocabulary chasm (+44.1% relative MRR@10 over BM25, p = 0.0002).

3

Engineering & Production Rigor: 100% consecutive Limon extraction (1,976 articles), zero test leakage, 136 passing tests, and external checkpoint hosting on Hugging Face Hub.

Limitations: Two statutory codes only; 200 test questions; OCR for scanned Labour Law is future work.

A6. Before Any Model: Reading the PDFs at All

Official government PDFs are not Unicode — this had to be solved first

The Problem

ODC's official PDFs are typeset in the legacy Limon font family. The text layer stores raw Latin keystrokes, e.g. maRta 336>- — a Unicode-aware extractor would produce meaningless text.

Khmer also writes pre-base vowels (េ ែ ៃ) and subscript consonants before the consonant they visually belong after, so a simple character map is not enough.

Our Fix

  • Byte-level Limon → Unicode character map, built from the corpus's own glyph tables
  • A small finite-state reordering pass that puts pre-base vowels and subscripts back into logical Unicode order
  • Result: 100% consecutive article extraction across all 1,976 articles (Civil 1–1304, Criminal 1–672), verified against the source PDFs
